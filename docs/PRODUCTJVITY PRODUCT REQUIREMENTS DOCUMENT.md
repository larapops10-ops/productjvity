**PRODUCT REQUIREMENTS DOCUMENT (PRD)**

**1\. Product Overview**

The product is a **financially backed accountability platform** that helps individuals and organisations turn goals and commitments into measurable outcomes.

Users commit to specific goals, agree to defined success criteria and, where applicable, place money at stake. The platform provides structure, reminders, progress tracking and verification. Users who successfully complete their commitments receive their committed funds and/or a reward, while users who fail to meet the agreed requirements may forfeit a predetermined portion of their commitment.

The platform can also be provided as an **accountability infrastructure service** to institutions that want to create and operate their own accountability programmes for their members, employees, students, customers or communities.

The fundamental principle is:

**Commit → Act → Verify → Complete → Reward**

Where a user does not complete the commitment:

**Commit → Fail → Forfeit → Allocate**

The forfeited amount may contribute to rewards for successful users, with a disclosed portion retained by the platform as breakage revenue.

**2\. Product Objectives**

The product should:

1. Help people follow through on meaningful goals and commitments.
2. Create stronger accountability through financial commitment and measurable consequences.
3. Make goals specific, measurable and time-bound.
4. Provide users with continuous visibility of their progress.
5. Reward users who successfully complete their commitments.
6. Create a sustainable economic model through transaction, institutional, API and breakage-related fees.
7. Enable institutions to create and manage accountability programmes without building the underlying infrastructure themselves.
8. Provide a trusted and transparent system for commitment, verification, reward and forfeiture.
9. Encourage successful completion rather than benefiting primarily from failure.

**3\. Target Users**

**3.1 Individual Users**

Individuals who want to achieve personal, professional, financial, educational, spiritual, health, lifestyle or other measurable goals.

Examples include:

- Completing a course or professional certification.
- Saving towards a defined objective.
- Completing a training programme.
- Developing a regular habit.
- Completing a defined number of activities within a period.
- Participating in a structured challenge.
- Meeting personal development objectives.

**3.2 Institutional Users**

Organisations that want to create and manage accountability programmes for groups of people.

Potential institutional users include:

- Churches and religious organisations.
- Schools and universities.
- Training and educational providers.
- Employers.
- Professional organisations.
- Communities and membership organisations.
- Financial and business platforms.
- Coaching and personal development organisations.

**3.3 Platform Administrator**

The platform owner/operator responsible for administering the overall service, managing institutional relationships, programme structures, financial rules, verification arrangements and platform operations.

**4\. Core Problem**

People regularly set goals and make commitments but fail to follow through because of:

- Procrastination.
- Lack of accountability.
- Weak incentives.
- Lack of structure.
- Poor visibility of progress.
- Absence of meaningful consequences.
- Difficulty measuring completion objectively.

Institutions face a related problem. They may want to run structured accountability programmes but lack the tools to manage participant commitments, progress, verification, rewards and consequences at scale.

The product addresses these problems by creating a **structured accountability system in which commitment has measurable consequences and successful performance has tangible rewards**.

**5\. Core Product Concept**

Every accountability programme should be built around five core elements:

**5.1 Commitment**

The user agrees to achieve a clearly defined objective within a defined period.

**5.2 Stake**

Where applicable, the user commits a specified amount of money to the programme.

**5.3 Measurement**

The programme establishes clear and measurable criteria for determining whether the user has succeeded.

**5.4 Verification**

The platform or authorised institution verifies whether the user has satisfied the applicable requirements.

**5.5 Outcome**

The user receives the applicable return and/or reward if successful. If unsuccessful, the predetermined consequence is applied.

**6\. Main User Journey**

**6.1 Discover**

The user should be able to discover available accountability programmes or create a personal commitment.

The user should understand:

- What the goal is.
- What is required.
- How long it will take.
- What amount is required, if any.
- What constitutes success.
- What constitutes failure.
- What reward is available.
- What amount may be forfeited.
- How the forfeited amount will be allocated.

All material terms should be clearly presented before the user commits.

**6.2 Create or Join a Commitment**

The user selects or creates a goal and agrees to:

- The objective.
- The timeframe.
- The milestones or requirements.
- The verification method.
- The financial commitment.
- The reward structure.
- The consequence for non-completion.

The user then confirms the commitment.

**6.3 Active Commitment**

During the commitment period, the user should be able to:

- View their goal.
- View the deadline.
- See progress.
- See completed and outstanding requirements.
- Receive reminders.
- Receive encouragement and accountability prompts.
- Submit evidence where required.
- Understand what remains to be completed.

**6.4 Verification**

At relevant milestones or at the end of the programme, the user's performance should be assessed against the predefined success criteria.

Verification should be based on the rules established when the user entered the commitment.

The rules should not be changed retrospectively in a manner that disadvantages the user.

**6.5 Completion**

Where the user satisfies the requirements:

- The commitment is marked successful.
- The user receives the applicable return.
- The user receives any applicable reward.
- The user's successful commitment is recorded in their history.

**6.6 Non-Completion**

Where the user does not satisfy the requirements:

- The commitment is marked unsuccessful.
- The applicable forfeiture is calculated according to the rules accepted at the beginning.
- The forfeited amount is allocated in accordance with the programme rules.
- The user can view the outcome and applicable allocation.

**7\. Institutional User Journey**

An institution should be able to:

**7.1 Create a Programme**

Define:

- Programme name.
- Objective.
- Target participants.
- Start and end dates.
- Eligibility requirements.
- Commitment requirements.
- Success criteria.
- Verification process.
- Reward structure.
- Forfeiture rules.
- Allocation of forfeited amounts.

**7.2 Enrol Participants**

The institution should be able to invite or register participants individually or in groups.

Participants should be able to understand and accept the programme rules before joining.

**7.3 Manage the Programme**

The institution should be able to:

- Monitor enrolment.
- Monitor participation.
- View progress.
- Send reminders.
- Communicate with participants.
- Review submitted evidence.
- Verify completion where applicable.
- Manage exceptions in accordance with established rules.

**7.4 Review Results**

At the end of the programme, the institution should be able to view:

- Number of participants.
- Number of successful participants.
- Number of unsuccessful participants.
- Completion rate.
- Amount committed.
- Amount returned.
- Rewards distributed.
- Forfeitures applied.
- Applicable platform fees.

**8\. Rewards and Breakage Model**

This is a central component of the product.

Where a user fails to meet the agreed requirements, the user may forfeit a predetermined portion of their commitment.

The forfeited amount may form a **Breakage Pool**.

The Breakage Pool may then be allocated between:

1. **Successful Users** — as additional rewards for completing their commitments.
2. **Platform** — as a disclosed breakage/platform fee.
3. **Institution or Programme Sponsor** — where the programme structure permits this.

The allocation should be established and disclosed **before users commit**.

**Example**

A user commits ₦100,000.

The programme provides for a maximum 50% forfeiture.

If the user fails:

- ₦50,000 may become subject to forfeiture.
- A defined portion may be added to the successful users' reward pool.
- A defined portion may constitute the platform's breakage revenue.

The exact percentages should be determined by the programme rules and clearly disclosed before commitment.

The platform's commercial model should not depend on encouraging users to fail. Its primary objective should remain **successful completion and user value**.

**9\. Business Model**

The platform may earn revenue through:

**9.1 Breakage Fees**

A disclosed portion of amounts forfeited by unsuccessful users.

**9.2 Transaction Fees**

Fees associated with qualifying financial transactions, including deposits, withdrawals, rewards and settlements, where applicable.

**9.3 Institutional Fees**

Subscription or programme-management fees paid by institutions.

**9.4 Programme Fees**

Fees charged for creating or operating specific accountability programmes.

**9.5 API/Infrastructure Fees**

Fees paid by organisations using the accountability infrastructure within their own products or services.

**9.6 Enterprise Fees**

Custom pricing for large organisations requiring specialised programmes, integrations, reporting or administrative capabilities.

**9.7 Verification Fees**

Applicable fees for verification services where such services involve an additional cost.

**10\. Accountability Programme Rules**

Every programme should have clearly defined rules before participants commit.

The rules should specify:

- What the participant must achieve.
- When the commitment begins and ends.
- How progress is measured.
- What evidence is required.
- How evidence is verified.
- What constitutes successful completion.
- What constitutes non-completion.
- The amount at risk.
- The maximum possible forfeiture.
- How rewards are calculated.
- How the Breakage Pool is allocated.
- Platform fees.
- What happens in exceptional circumstances.
- The process for resolving disputes.

Users should be required to accept these rules before making a commitment.

**11\. Personal Accountability Programmes**

The product should allow an individual to create a personal accountability commitment without necessarily joining an institutional programme.

The user should be able to:

1. Define a goal.
2. Set a deadline.
3. Define measurable success criteria.
4. Select the financial commitment.
5. Determine the applicable consequence.
6. Track progress.
7. Receive reminders.
8. Submit evidence where necessary.
9. Complete the commitment.
10. Receive the applicable outcome.

**12\. Social and Group Accountability**

The product should support group-based accountability.

Users may participate in programmes where several people pursue the same or related goals.

Examples include:

- 30-day challenges.
- Savings challenges.
- Study challenges.
- Professional certification challenges.
- Fitness or lifestyle challenges.
- Church or community challenges.
- Employee development challenges.

The product should allow appropriate visibility of group progress while respecting individual privacy.

**13\. Notifications and Accountability**

The product should provide timely accountability communications, including:

- Commitment confirmation.
- Upcoming deadlines.
- Missed milestones.
- Progress reminders.
- Encouragement.
- Verification requests.
- Completion confirmation.
- Reward notifications.
- Forfeiture notifications.
- Programme announcements.

Notifications should help users remain accountable without becoming excessive or intrusive.

**14\. User Dashboard**

The individual user should be able to see:

- Active commitments.
- Progress towards each commitment.
- Upcoming deadlines.
- Completed commitments.
- Unsuccessful commitments.
- Amount currently committed.
- Potential amount at risk.
- Rewards earned.
- Historical performance.
- Available programmes.

The institutional dashboard should provide a corresponding overview of its programmes and participants.

**15\. Transparency**

Because money is involved, transparency is a core product requirement.

Before committing, users should be able to clearly understand:

- How much they are committing.
- How much they may lose.
- How success is determined.
- How failure is determined.
- What they receive if successful.
- What happens to forfeited funds.
- What portion goes to successful users.
- What portion is retained by the platform.
- Any other applicable fees.

There should be no material financial condition that is hidden from the user.

**16\. Disputes and Exceptions**

The product should provide a process for situations such as:

- Incorrect verification.
- Technical or operational failure affecting a commitment.
- Disputed completion status.
- Incorrect application of a forfeiture.
- Exceptional circumstances recognised under the programme rules.
- Payment or settlement discrepancies.

The applicable dispute process should be communicated before commitment.

**17\. User History and Reputation**

The product should maintain a user's history of commitments, including:

- Commitments created.
- Commitments completed.
- Commitments not completed.
- Rewards received.
- Programmes participated in.
- Completion performance over time.

Where appropriate, users may be able to build an **accountability profile** showing their history of successful commitments.

Any public or institutional use of such information should be subject to appropriate user consent and programme rules.

**18\. Platform Administration**

The platform administrator should be able to:

- Manage institutional accounts.
- Approve or suspend programmes.
- Configure programme categories and available structures.
- Manage platform-wide rules.
- Monitor financial activity.
- Monitor rewards and forfeitures.
- Manage disputes and exceptions.
- Manage institutional administrators.
- Monitor platform usage.
- Review operational reports.
- Maintain appropriate records of significant administrative actions.

**19\. Key Product Principles**

The product should be built around the following principles:

**Clarity**

Users should understand exactly what they are committing to.

**Measurability**

Goals should have objective and understandable success criteria.

**Accountability**

Commitments should create meaningful incentives to follow through.

**Fairness**

Users should not be subjected to undisclosed or retrospective conditions.

**Transparency**

Financial consequences and reward allocation should be visible before commitment.

**Positive Incentives**

The product should primarily encourage successful completion rather than profit from failure.

**Trust**

Users should have confidence that commitments, verification, rewards and forfeitures are administered according to predetermined rules.

**Flexibility**

The infrastructure should support different types of accountability programmes and institutional use cases.

**20\. Success Metrics**

The success of the product should be measured by:

- Number of active users.
- Number of active institutions.
- Number of programmes created.
- Number of commitments made.
- Commitment completion rate.
- Repeat participation rate.
- Total value committed.
- Total rewards distributed.
- User retention.
- Institutional retention.
- Revenue generated from platform fees.
- Revenue generated from breakage.
- Percentage of users who successfully complete multiple commitments.
- Number and resolution rate of disputes.

A particularly important product metric should be the **successful completion rate**, because the core purpose of the platform is to help users achieve their commitments.

**21\. Product Vision**

The long-term vision is to build a **global accountability infrastructure** that allows individuals and organisations to put meaningful commitments behind their goals.

Rather than simply reminding people what they said they would do, the platform creates a system where:

**Goals become commitments.  
Commitments become measurable.  
Measurement creates accountability.  
Accountability drives completion.  
Completion creates rewards.**

The platform can ultimately provide this infrastructure directly to individuals or power accountability programmes operated by third-party institutions and applications.
